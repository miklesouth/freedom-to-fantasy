1 The Point

A fun season where everybody stays in it week to week, even if your playoff hopes die in October. Set your lineup every week, make honest moves, talk a little trash in the league chat, and don't ghost your team. That's the deal.

The golden rule: If your team goes inactive — stale lineup, injured guys left in, byes unfilled — you're not just hurting yourself, you're handing a free win to whoever you play. Don't be that manager. Life happens and a missed week is forgiven; disappearing for the season is akin to inviting your best friend over for dinner and then driving to Savannah just before they arrive, where hopefully you'll be eaten by an alligator as cosmic justice.

3 The Weekly Mystery Pot — how it works

Each week, one rule from the master list wins that week's $10. The catch: the rule stays secret until halftime of Monday Night Football. By then almost every game is over and your lineup is long locked — so there's no way to chase a specific rule. You just set your honest best lineup, and the mystery rule rewards whoever happened to fit it that week.

The spin: at MNF halftime, that week's designated spinner spins the online Mystery Rule Spinner and posts the result to the league. The winner is then tallied once the Monday night game ends.

🎡 Live tools: Mystery Rule Spinner · preseason practice wheel

Who spins? We rotate — everyone gets a turn. A set rotation (below) means each manager gets the spotlight at least once and has a reason to tune in. If a person can't make it, the commissioner spins on their behalf.

5 Last Place & the Consolation Ladder

Because fear of finishing last is the best motivator of all.

The teams that miss the 6-team playoff aren't done — they drop into the consolation ladder and keep playing. Whoever finishes dead last takes home the Freedom to Fantasy Last-Place Trophy — an appropriately ridiculous traveling trophy — and keeps it on display until next season, complete with a commemorative photo for the league chat.

9 The Fine Print

Edge cases so nobody argues on a Monday night.

Ties. Any weekly mystery prize that ends in a tie is split evenly among those tied.

Week 1. "Most Improved" isn't eligible in Week 1 (no prior week to improve on) — if the spinner lands there in Week 1, spin again.

Rules that need a projection. A few rules ("David vs. Goliath," "Boom Goes the Dynamite," "Exactly As Planned") use ESPN's pregame projections, which ESPN shows on the matchup and box-score screens. The commissioner reads them off after the games.

All-play rules. "Highway Robbery" and "Bad Beat" compare your score against all nine other teams that week — a one-minute check the commissioner does from the weekly scoreboard.

Inactive lineups. ESPN does not auto-fix lineups, so an empty or injured slot just scores zero. The commissioner will drop a "set your lineups" reminder in the chat each week, but ultimately it's on each manager.

Changing the rules. Anything in this constitution can be changed by a simple majority vote of the league — best done before the draft, not mid-season.
